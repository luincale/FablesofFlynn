Part I — The Story
A Machine Older Than the Sword Coast
Long before Waterdeep raised its towers, the empire of Netheril floated in the sky, lifted by
mythallars — great orbs of raw magical energy. The youngest and most gifted mage ever named
to the Netherese High Council, Archmage Carrolyn del Thristen, earned his fame by building a

-- 1 of 29 --

mythallar powerful enough to raise the floating city of Larkspur with only a single death to pay
for it.
Del Thristen's closest companion was no mortal. Dante Saturnid, the Orphan Prince of Dreams
and Archfey of Sweet Slumber, had been cursed by the Queen of Air and Darkness into the shape
of a child and exiled to a Netherese boarding school. There the fey trickster and the human
prodigy became lifelong colleagues — Dante teaching Carrolyn illusions and dreams, Carrolyn
drawing Dante deeper into Netherese magecraft and politics.
Not everyone trusted the mythallars. The proud House of Wingsong — warriors and wizards
both, a mortal noble line — warned that the orbs led to evils the empire could not survive. For
saying so they were quietly exiled from Larkspur before Karsus ever worked his folly, driven to
the empire's western borders, where they befriended the elves of Neverwinter Wood and raised
Wingsong Manor as their seat. Their warning proved true. In –339 DR, Karsus's Folly shattered
Netheril, and Larkspur fell from the sky. (Per the Candlekeep Annals: the archwizard Karsus
reached for the power of the goddess of magic herself; she gave her life rather than let him hold it,
and was reborn as Mystra. Arcane magic itself briefly died with her — which the Annals name as
the true cause of Netheril's fall from the sky.)
The Making of a Prison
Del Thristen survived, and with Dante's help he led the shattered survivors of Larkspur west — to
Wingsong Manor, the one roof still standing that could shelter them. What began as a plea for
refuge did not stay one for long. Over the following decade the archmage's ambition outgrew his
gratitude: by –328 DR he had besieged the manor outright, claimed it for his own, and begun
repurposing it, stone and spell, into what history would come to call Wingsong Tower. Dante's
illusions hid the transformed keep from Netherese enemies while del Thristen secured the
surviving mythallar in its Anchor Chamber. What began as a refuge became, over decades, a
horror of the archmage's own making.
He built the Infinite Planar Cycler — a machine that fed on the "dipolar" energies of two
demiplanes he tore open: a Feywild side and a Shadowfell side. To power it he needed two living
poles. Dante, his oldest friend, became the light pole, forced into a deep slumber. For the dark pole
he raised the Macroscian — a "Long Shadow," a skeleton enthralled by necromancy and fed
living flesh until it swelled into a ravenous monstrosity. Between these two anchored beings, the
tower produced a limitless pool of mana — and began flickering endlessly through planes and
time.
In his siege of the manor, del Thristen murdered its defender, Lord Val'Orea Wingsong — the
mortal head of her house, not the couatl she was once believed to be. (Emporium Conservatory

archives: this was a common early misreading of the Wingsong Tower record, since a band of
couatl-blooded monks were turned away from the gates that very same year, seeking their lost
lord. But those monks — the ancient order that would become the Knights of Wingsong — had
been in commission long before House Wingsong ever existed, an order of Jazirian's servants that
only later took the family's name and cause as their own. Val'Orea, whatever grace or favor she
carried, was flesh and blood like the rest of her line.) He turned the monks away and told them
their lord was dead. He fought off the Shadovar when the goddess Shar sent them hunting the
tower, then sealed its doors forever. (Per the Candlekeep Annals: Shar sheltered a whole
Netherese enclave, Thultanthar — the City of Shade — within the Shadowfell at the empire's fall.
Its Shadovar people would not return openly to Faerûn for well over a thousand years, which the
Conservatory's scholars believe explains why Shar's interest in Wingsong Tower went quiet for so
long.) By –250 DR he had lost control of his own creation; the tower's warring magics drove him
mad, and across the centuries he decayed into a Nothic, scribbling mad ramblings into journals no
one would read for a thousand years.
Flynn and the Wish
Centuries later, a wizard out of Neverwinter named Finnegan Flynn was building a dream of his
own: the Emporium of Wonders, a place to collect and study all magic. Roughly five years
before the present, the Lord's Alliance — through Sildar Hallwinter — called him south of
Waterdeep to face the Circle of Flame, the cult of the fire genasi sorceress Hariqua Na'Haran,
illegitimate daughter of the Efreeti Sultan of the City of Brass.
Flynn brought his three original companions — Damien Wingsong, Zoey Drake, and Bastion
Drake — and named the band "the Emporium." They cut through the cult and drove Hariqua into
a final duel amid fire and storm on the Trollclaws. But at the decisive moment, Flynn halted.
Knowing Hariqua's blood carried the reality-binding power of a wish, he traded her freedom —
and one wish each from his companions folded into his own — for a single, immense wish,
granted atop the hills that very day. What he asked for he has never plainly named — and may not
fully know it himself. Publicly, Hariqua "fled" and the cult was "broken." Flynn's reward was a
Waterdeep barracks, which he transformed into the Emporium of Wonders and, within five years,
into a world-renowned power.
The bargain came due. Back in the City of Brass, the Sultan judged Hariqua weak for trading a
wish for her own life. He stripped her efreeti mantle, exiled her to suffer hunger and burns she had
never known, then restored her power with a command: "Conquer Flynn and make Waterdeep
suffer." She returned to the material plane with a cauldron of everlasting flame and began
rebuilding.

The Fables Assemble
The present tale follows a newer band — the Fables — recruited into Flynn's orbit.
Prologue I – The Oni of Tyl Myerda: Bastion hired the halfling rogue Ty'Ny De'Ath and a
human fighter. They seized a pirate ship (gaining a strange bird companion), sailed to the
magic-banning isle of Tyl Myerda, slew an Oni masquerading as the Lord Justice, and secured
a provisions contract for the Emporium.
Prologue II – Hunting Flames: Ty'Ny recruited the blue-skinned druid Kaladin Tempest.
Together they destroyed a Cult of Flame acolyte cell and its summoned salamander near
Inndale, delivering the corpse to the Emporium for study.
Then came Book One. The Fables were formally hired and met Flynn's Three Companions. In
Phandalin they broke the Redbrands, unmasked Iarno "Glasstaff" Albrek (mind-controlled by
the drow Nezznar, the Black Spider), rescued Sildar Hallwinter, and located the lost Mines of
Phandelver. In Wave Echo Cave they wrested the mine from the Black Spider outright.
At the Soulfire Forge, the Fables met Hariqua Na'Haran herself and her reborn Cult of Flame —
an encounter bound up with the tragedy at Thornhold Keep. Hariqua's assault fell first on the
keep's dwarven garrison, the shield dwarves who had held Thornhold's underground halls for
generations; she slaughtered them where they stood, meaning to claim the fortress above as her
own. The Fables and the Emporium arrived in time to break her siege — not in time to save the
dwarves. (Per the Candlekeep Annals: those were the shield dwarves of Clan Stoneshaft, who had
dwelt in caverns beneath Thornhold — reached by a passage into the Underdark — since long
before the keep changed hands to the Zhentarim in 1368 DR. That their long stewardship should
end at a fire genasi's hand struck the Conservatory's scholars as a bitter, final page to a very old
story.) With Hariqua driven off and no dwarves left to reclaim their ancestral hold, the Emporium's
claim to Thornhold went uncontested.
Once the Fables had battled Hariqua to a standstill and sent her fleeing back to the Fire Plane,
Flynn himself arrived — and in a rare display of his own considerable might, laid low the hidden
caverns of the Circle of Flame outright. He claimed the Soulfire Forge itself as a prize of war, an
artifact of fire magic potent enough to be worth a wizard's personal attention. They chased the
necromancer Hamun Kost, a Red Wizard of Thay who had murdered Moira'Val's master Peer'Na
to steal a book studying Wingsong Tower and a species of firefly that could reveal it. Kost now
rots in Thornhold's dungeons; his patron, they learned, had been Halia Thornton of the
Zhentarim.

Into the Dark — and Bastion's Ledger
The hunt for the tower drove the Fables into the Underdark, chasing the fireflies before the
Zhentarim could. It is at this juncture that Bastion's private notes to Flynn capture the
Emporium's true, unvarnished situation:
Hariqua has retreated to the Fire Plane and the Sultan's court; Damien's study of her soul-
powered forge confirms its "devilish" nature. Making an enemy of the entire Fire Plane would
be ruinous — Bastion counsels a subtler path and has the Harpers listening.
The Zhentarim and the Red Wizards of Thay are racing the Emporium for the same prize.
Halia is confirmed dead; her successor is unknown. The Black Network wants to buy the
Emporium's loyalty or seize it whole — and its hunger for Wingsong Tower forces the
Emporium to move against it.
Thornhold's reconstruction proceeds under a Lord's Alliance lease, but the cost in dwarven
lives — the Stoneshaft garrison Hariqua put to the sword — was steep, the Wimbleblats are
turning Waterdeep against them, and Baldur's Gate has soured (Neverwinter still loves them).
The Emporium is expanding along the Sword Coast and Triboar Trail toward five times its
Waterdeep capacity. Zoey Drake and Gypsa are below decks overseeing the engineering of
the captured Soulfire Forge into the flying ship — no mystery, simply occupied. Bastion's
real appeal to Flynn is for help managing Elminster and Lady Silverhand, whose scrutiny of
the Emporium is mounting. "I may need your help."
In the Underdark they found Hideaway Bay, the last deep gnomes of drowned Tingstone. In
exchange for a new home at Thundertree, the gnomes shared the firefly lore — and the brilliant
young engineer Gypsa would go on to captain the Fables' skyship.
But Thundertree had to be won first. The ruined village was held by a young green dragon —
one of Hariqua Na'Haran's own cult allies. (Emporium Conservatory archives, filed by Beratrymus
Villium: Thundertree stood empty and wild since Mount Hotenow's eruption devastated the
Neverwinter region a generation past — precisely the kind of abandoned ruin a young dragon
would claim as its own. The Conservatory's notes even venture a name for the beast: Venomfang.)
With the region already calmer after the Fables' earlier rout of the Zhentarim's grip on Phandalin,
the druid Kaladin Tempest led the retaking of Thundertree, and the Fables slew the dragon. The
victory both gave the deep gnomes a peaceful place to settle and cut away one of the Circle of
Flame's alliances.
Plague, Tower, and Return
At Larkspur Heath, the Fables found the great Library sealed by Sir Bently Rhammer on the
orders of Lord Neebo Wimbleblat, its librarians dying of a mystery poison. The confrontation

turned to slaughter: Rhammer fell, the disgraced ex-chancellor Newton Filz was exposed as a
Zhentarim agent and killed, and the poison was traced to Wimbleblat-marked supplies. The
librarians were healed and raised. (Per the Candlekeep Annals: raising the dead has lately been no
simple matter across Faerûn — a death curse elsewhere in the world has been known to turn
resurrection magic hollow. That the Larkspur librarians rose at all, the Conservatory notes, may
say something quiet about Flynn's own hand in the working.)
At last the Fables reached Wingsong Tower. They passed through its Fey and Shadow demiplanes
and its Anchor Chamber, and there vanquished the Macroscian — the Long Shadow — to close
the Shadowfell tear. The moment it toppled, something in the chamber changed: an apparition rose
where the creature had stood, wearing Val'Orea Wingsong's face. (Emporium Conservatory
archives: the likeliest theory — never confirmed, only ever offered — is that some fragment of
Val'Orea's memory had been trapped within the Macroscian's own gestations since the night it
killed her, and was only loosed when the thing was finally destroyed.) The figure was no ghost of
Val'Orea herself, though — it was the Raven Queen, borrowing that face to speak. She explained
little of herself or her purposes, but she gave two things freely. First, a truth: that both Moira'Val
and Pim carried Wingsong blood in their lineage, kin by a line neither had known to claim.
Second, a gift: a cloak she named the Mantle of the Raven Queen, bestowed on Ty'Ny — and
with it, a charge rather than a command. She asked the halfling rogue to uphold her ethos: to slay
the undead, and to stand against any shadow that would bend death's power to infernal ends. Then,
almost in passing, she asked one more thing — that the Fables send her greetings to Flynn, the
next time they spoke with him, in the easy tone of someone who had known him a very long time.
The mad archmage del Thristen finally met his end at the hands of his oldest friend, Dante, now
awakened as Pim's fey companion. The Fables escaped with the Mythallar of ancient Larkspur —
and though the Zhentarim, encircling the tower, seized Lord Damien Wingsong and two
Knights of Wingsong, the Fables broke the encirclement and freed them, carrying all three safely
home. It is Damien and his knights — the ancient couatl order sworn to the tower — who carry
couatl nature; Moira'Val and Pim, by contrast, descend from the mortal Wingsong bloodline
itself, and Moira'Val now carries its birthright.
The Knights, the Bloodline, and Who Flynn Really Is
Two conversations reframe everything the Fables thought they knew.
Speaking with Moira'Val, Damien admitted he does not carry Wingsong blood at all. The Order
of the Knights of Wingsong — an ancient couatl order sworn to Jazirian's cause — had stood
watch for a thousand years before House Wingsong ever existed, and only later took the family's
name and purpose as its own. When Damien came to believe the true bloodline had finally died

-- 6 of 29 --

out in the long shadow of Karsus's Folly, he took up the Wingsong name and its two charges
himself, out of devotion rather than birthright: first, to gather and keep the Mythallars safe;
second, to protect and serve Flynn. He does not yet know that a true heir still walks beside him
— for Flynn has appeared throughout history as different people, Damien revealed, not quite
reincarnation, he said, but close enough. The roughly 110 years he'll own to are how long he has
worn the name Damien Wingsong and this dragonborn shape — not how long he has kept his
vigil, nor how long he has been utterly devoted to Flynn, trusting him to guard the world from the
Mythallars' power.
Then, upon the Fables' return, Flynn offered clarity of his own — and it was staggering. The
Mythallars, he explained, were never merely engines of flight. In building them the archmagi of
Netheril (and their elven counterparts) cleaved the very fabric of magic, matter, and time, fraying
the Weave with every cut. That same power let them lock the faery prince Dante in eternal sleep
and lift cities into the sky — and it tore open a loophole in the Pact Primordial, the ancient
agreement of creation, for Asmodeus to exploit.
Flynn confessed his true nature: he is not a man but a living dream of Jazirian, the primordial
couatl-deity of law, light, and mercy — a figment sent to walk the world across many lives and
learn from its passions. Asmodeus — who also hides beneath the name Ahriman — is Jazirian's
twin sibling, now sitting injured in the pits of Baator, scheming to unmake the world and
refashion the broken Pact to his sole will. Shar, Flynn says, is Asmodeus's shadow; Jazirian has
such shadows too. The Knights of Wingsong are only the most recent of those who have learned to
seek Flynn out across the ages — before them, the cloaked wizards of a certain Neverwinter tower
(now Owl's Roost).
Worst of all: when Hariqua Na'Haran tried to twist Flynn's Wish to her own ends, she
unwittingly re-forged a bond of patronage between Flynn and his fiendish sibling. Flynn now fears
he has pulled Asmodeus deeper into his designs than is safe — that the archfiend shares once more
in power that was long ago split between them.
And Flynn confessed what the Wish had truly been for. Its exact workings are clouded — veiled,
perhaps, by divine power or by the laws primordial themselves — but its intent he could name: he
had wished for access to Ahriman's own power, gambling that a measure of his brother's strength
would let him act faster than the archfiend and mend the Pact before Asmodeus could seize it. He
had not gone in unprepared: for years, in a private research dimension, he had studied fiends,
devils, and beholders — beings whose powers run closest to his brother's — both to ready himself
for the Wish and to learn how the Negative Energy Plane might warp the gifts of one who, as a
dream of Jazirian, is tied to pure positive energy. Yet something did not work as intended. Flynn
now suspects a missing fulcrum to the twinned powers he and his brother embody — some

-- 7 of 29 --

balancing point that neither of them yet holds. For the moment the bond cuts strangely in his
favor: while the Wish links the siblings for the first time in an epoch, it also keeps Flynn hidden
from Ahriman's devilish sight. And in the space between the two of them, Flynn senses
something — or someone — foreboding, standing unseen between the brothers.
Flynn's wager, and his hope, is that neither he nor his sibling remain the beings who first made
that pact: that Asmodeus has become more archfiend than creator, and Jazirian more archangel of
healing than cold law. He urges the Emporium to move with urgency — but not haste — to mend
the Pact before their enemies grasp what is truly at stake.
Bastion, meanwhile, lays out the choices ahead, naming five gathering storms: a chaotic gray
mist over the Silver Marches; the sudden emergence of the Zhentarim's leader, the Peregost;
the Well of Dragons gone active, its smoke blinding the Emporium's many eyes; Baldur's Gate
drowning in blood and murder as the names of the Dead Three spread and war with Amn looms
to the south; and Shar, dancing at the very edge of sight for reasons no one can yet fathom.
Aboard the flaming, battered Fables' Fortune, under Gypsa's piloting, the band returns at last to
Thornhold — now dwarfed by Flynn's ever-growing citadel. With them ride the rescued Lord
Damien Wingsong and his two knights — couatls of the ancient order that bears the Wingsong
name, freed from the Zhentarim. They carry more than the Mythallar: Ty'Ny bears the Mantle of
the Raven Queen and her charge to hunt the undead and the shadow that wields them; Moira'Val
carries the Wingsong birthright, and knows now that Pim shares her blood; Salihafa hears his god
clearly; Kaladin wields new elemental mastery (and the Mythallar itself, riding in a druid's
rucksack); and Dante travels as a great moth beside Pim. Behind them drift fell winds — Shar's
gaze, and the Zhentarim seeking their arrest in the name of Waterdeep's nobility. Zoey and Bastion
wait at Flynn's side, and the search for the next lost Mythallar is only beginning.
Four Hands, One Wonder: How Flynn, Damien, Bastion, and Zoey Built the
Emporium
With the barracks bought and the doors of the Emporium of Wonders newly hung, Flynn had a
vision fixed in his mind — a place to collect and study all magic, and, in time, a citadel worthy of
guarding it. A vision, however grand, cannot mortar itself, negotiate its own trade routes, stand a
watch, or hold a household together. That work fell, as it always would, to the three who had
followed him out of Neverwinter and never once needed to be asked twice — though one of them,
in truth, had been following him a great deal longer than that.
Damien was never meant to recruit mercenaries or sign trade agreements, and never has. What he
brought to the Emporium cannot be itemized in Bastion's ledgers or drawn on Zoey's fortification
plans, though the whole enterprise leans on it more than either: heart, and loyalty, and a fairness

-- 8 of 29 --

that has never once bent under pressure from either side of an argument. He is fair and kind,
strong and gentle, and he commands the Knights of Wingsong with that same impossible balance
— a knight commander who has never needed to raise his voice to be obeyed, because his knights
have simply never doubted that his judgment is sound. He is, by a wide margin, the oldest of
Flynn's three companions. Those who spend enough time in his company come away with the
quiet, half-spoken suspicion that he holds more wisdom and lived experience than even the
wondrous Flynn — a suspicion none of them can quite prove, and none of them are wrong to
have. The roughly hundred and ten years he'll admit to, if pressed, are how long he has worn this
name and this dragonborn shape. They are not how long he has been watching.
For Damien found Flynn before Flynn was born this time. He and his secret couatl knighthood had
been watching the potential for longer than any mortal record could easily account for, and it was
Damien and his Knights who stood guard the night Flynn was reborn into the world he now walks.
What that night held, and what it cost, is a story for another time — Damien has never told it in
full even to Bastion or Zoey, and Flynn himself, when the subject rises, only ever goes quiet and
lets his oldest companion decide how much of it deserves daylight. It is enough, for now, to know
that Damien's loyalty to Flynn did not begin at a barracks in Merchant Square. It began before
there was a Flynn there to be loyal to, and has never once wavered since.
That same patient devotion is what actually raised Bastion and Zoey. Flynn never formally
adopted them — they kept the Drake name, their own family's name, and are proudly no one's
wards — but he funded their private education in Neverwinter and, now that both have grown into
their thirty-second year, treats them as members of his chosen family and equals in full. It was
Damien, though, who was present for the raising itself — who taught a young, restless Zoey that
strength without restraint is just another kind of recklessness, and taught a cleverer, more slippery
Bastion that charm spent without loyalty behind it eventually charms no one at all. The twins seem
to have inherited a piece of each man for it: Flynn's endless youthfulness and flair show in both of
them still, more than three decades in, while it's Damien's reserve and quiet strength that surfaces
the moment either twin stops performing and starts deciding something that actually matters.
Neither twin would put any of this in quite those words. Both of them, without exception, still go
to Damien first when a decision actually frightens them, and both know better than to lie to him
about why.
Flynn gave the vision and the coin, and — rarely, but memorably — his own considerable might:
the caverns of the Circle of Flame laid low in a single afternoon; a formal charter of protection
handed to the resettled gnomes of Thundertree with no strings attached; a Waterdeep barracks
transformed, in five years, into a name spoken with the same weight as Waterdeep or Neverwinter
themselves. He is the reason any of it was possible. He is also, by his own household's quiet

agreement, the one member of it least suited to actually building anything — a fact Damien has
known, and gently forgiven him for, since long before Flynn knew it about himself.
Bastion built the trade. He doesn't advertise; he notices — the dwarven stoneworkers of a failing
Yartar quarry before their guild could scatter them to worse employers, the glaziers of a burned-
out Waterdeep workshop starving for a patron, the smiths who could work Netherese-touched ore
without it souring in their hands. Each got the same pitch over the same excellent wine: not a job,
but a place in something that would outlast them. The same instinct built the Emporium's whole
regional web. In Phandalin, first proof of concept, he brokered the claim on Barthen's old
warehouse, funded the Phandelver Field Research Outpost, and quietly underwrote Toblen
Stonehill's rise to council chair. Along the Triboar Trail's ruined stretch at Conyberry, a handful of
resettled families got a stipend for a mill and a well in exchange for nothing but a waystation
flying no flag. At Triboar itself he made himself indispensable to two rival towns at once —
Triboar and long-quarreling Yartar both — offering each the same deal and letting neither know
the other had it too; it was here, chasing Hamun Kost's trail, that the Fables first saw how far his
paper trail already reached ahead of them. Thundertree's deep gnomes became not just a rescue
but a trade partnership, their clockwork exported through Emporium hands to Waterdeep nobles
who have no idea where their finest mechanisms are actually made. Larkspur Heath's Library
was rebuilt as a sister house to the Emporium's own Atheneum, its supply line something Bastion
never lets run short. And Tyl Myerda, a magic-wary island, was won over by not being asked to
change at all — the Emporium helped formalize the isle's own laws rather than override them, and
the elders still speak of "Bastion the bard" fondly, with no particular reason to know he never
stopped working while he sang.
That same talent-gathering instinct built Southwall from bare ground in under two years — a real
village, not a construction camp, pressed against Thornhold's south wall at the foot of the thorn the
old keep crowns, close enough that on quiet nights its three hundred workers can hear the citadel's
masons still at labor above them. At its heart stands The Wayfarer, the town's true center of
gravity, run by a broad, easy-smiling man named Parker Peterson — found by Bastion keeping a
threadbare roadside inn on the Triboar Trail and simply asked, how would you like a bigger room
to be generous in? Parker said yes before the sentence finished, and every worker in Southwall
will tell you he's the finest innkeeper on the High Road. What they're less sure of is why the stout
never tastes quite the same twice, always somehow exactly what the room needed that night, or
who's really playing the music that never seems to stop. Parker doesn't know the tiefling who
charmed him out of his old life still visits some nights uninvited, and never once takes credit for
the song already playing when he sits down.
Zoey built the wall — and everything the wall was built to protect. Where her brother
meanders, Zoey strides with purpose; where Bastion charms an answer out of someone over an

-- 10 of 29 --

hour of wine, Zoey simply tells them the truth and lets it land. She is, by any honest account, the
equal of Bastion's talents and the stronger of the two twins besides — an engineer and a battle
tactician in the same body, which is a rarer combination than either skill alone. The Serpent
Mercenaries did not exist five years ago; she built their rank structure, their training, their
discipline from nothing, and it is her lieutenants, not Lord's Alliance agents, who answer the call
when trouble reaches Phandalin's Triboar Trail cutoff or the roads to Triboar and Yartar.
Thornhold's reconstruction may draw on Bastion's laborers, but its walls, its garrison rotations, its
sightlines and killing floors are Zoey's own design — a citadel is only as good as the people who
can hold it, and she has made sure Flynn's can be held.
Her sharpest triumph, though, she shares with a friend. Gypsa — the deep gnome engineer who
led the Fables out of the Underdark and now captains the Fables' Fortune — found in Zoey an
unlikely kindred spirit, and the two of them became fast friends practically the moment the ship's
keel needed real work done to it. Where Zoey is blunt and Gypsa is bright, the two fit together the
way a hammer fits a chisel; more than one Serpent has remarked that Gypsa's sheer, undimmable
joy has been the only tonic that reliably softens Zoey's edges, and that Zoey, in turn, is the only
person who takes Gypsa's wilder engineering schemes seriously enough to actually help her build
them. It was that partnership that turned Flynn's captured Soulfire Forge from a war trophy into
the Fortune's beating heart, retrofitted for raw firepower and flight speed neither of them could
have managed alone. Ask Zoey what she's proudest of building in five years, and she won't say the
walls. She'll say the ship, and she'll say Gypsa's name in the same breath without noticing she's
done it.
The Citadel Rising is what all four of them add up to. Thornhold is no longer merely leased
ground — it is the place where Flynn's vision, Damien's steadiness, Bastion's trade, and Zoey's
walls all converge into something none of them could have built apart. The old keep and its walls,
the Thornhold the Fables first found, sit eastward now, hunched and humble at the thorn's
shoulder, closest to the High Road and the travelers who still arrive that way — for the true
Citadel has been raised at the very peak of the promontory, out over the western cliff edge and the
sea below, until the original fortress looks less like a stronghold and more like a gatehouse to
something far greater. (Per the Candlekeep Annals: Thornhold's hill has always dropped away to
a seaside cliff on its western face, with the High Road running along its eastern approach — a
detail the Emporium's own builders kept faithfully when they raised the Citadel at the hill's
western peak, over the sea, rather than displacing the old keep that still guards the road.) Pale
stone rises in ribbed, scalloped domes dark as slate against the sky, crowned with spires hung with
long banners bearing the Emporium's ten-pointed star; grand stairways and terraced plazas step
down the cliff face from the citadel's crown all the way to Southwall's rooftops, so that a worker
leaving the Wayfarer at dusk can look west and watch Flynn's wonder catch the last of the light

before anything below does. Its reconstruction employs laborers drawn from every village up and
down the High Road, now drawing Emporium coin rather than a lord's. It houses the Serpent
Mercenaries' true seat, a standing force answerable to no crown, and the Knights of Wingsong
besides, watching over it all with the same patience Damien has always had.
Beneath it all, within the cliffs themselves, run the old passages and dwarven halls the Stoneshafts
once called home — secret ways Zoey has quietly re-warded and re-walled rather than sealed.
Most lead nowhere anymore. One still descends, uninterrupted, into the Underdark far below,
watched at all hours by a standing rotation of Zoey's own Serpent Mercenaries — a post she trusts
to no one else. And one other opens outward rather than down: a hidden cavern the tunnels break
open into, vast and unlit until torches catch it, its far wall simply gone — a sheer, open cliffside
looking out over the western sea, wide and tall enough to swallow the Fables' Fortune whole with
room to spare. It is here, behind stone no map admits to and a cliff face no ship's lookout would
ever think to search, that the Fables have made their true headquarters — closer to the Citadel's
heart than even Flynn's own chambers, and known to fewer people besides. Its own doors are
watched just as closely, though by different eyes: Glorn the spectator and his keeper Ben
Pendragon stand guard there, letting nothing pass that Flynn hasn't already forgiven. Within,
among the storerooms, a familiar face keeps the books: Gerk, once Phandalin's town clerk, left
that quiet post to become the Fables' own Quartermaster and banker, and has lived in the caverns
below the Citadel ever since — closer to the ledgers than to daylight, and happier for it. And
Flynn's citadel rises above the old keep's walls a little higher every season, not hidden, not
apologized for, simply there, the way Waterdeep's own towers are there.
None of it was seized. Every mile of it was given, freely, by people the Emporium helped when no
one else would. That is precisely what worries Elminster and Lady Silverhand more than any army
could. A conquered land can be liberated. A land that loves its conqueror has no reason to want to
be.
The Shadow: Wimbleblat's Confederation
Lord Neebo Wimbleblat did not survive Larkspur Heath by being reckless twice. House
Wimbleblat has stood among Waterdeep's magecraft nobility for generations — not so storied as
House Wands, whose towers still bear the founder's name, but old enough, and proud enough, that
its fortunes were built on the same currency: fine artifice, sold to a city that has always paid well
for wonders. The constructs Sir Bently Rhammer commanded at the Library's doors were
Wimbleblat make, every one — clever, beautiful, murderous work, utterly deniable once their
maker was safely back behind Sea Ward walls when the killing was traced. The killing was traced,
and Rhammer and Filz both paid for it. Neebo did not. He simply stopped fighting the war he'd
already lost and started the one he could win.

-- 12 of 29 --

Where the Circle of Flame's echo had been poison and constructs, Wimbleblat's second campaign
is paperwork, patronage, and the long memory of a class that never forgives being made to look
the fool by a man who "arrived from nowhere five years ago." He has built a confederation — old
houses whose fortunes have thinned in exact proportion to the Emporium's rise. House Wands,
whose magecraft once had no rival in the city and never fully recovered what the Spellplague
burned out of them, needs no convincing that a barracks-turned-museum has no business
outshining a house a thousand years older. Others in the confederation guard debts and secrets
Bastion suspects run considerably darker than wounded pride — soft talk of certain Sea Ward
galas where guests leave wealthier and stranger than they arrived, of a family whose children are
never quite seen in daylight. He has not confirmed it. He has not stopped listening either.
The confederation's weapons are entirely legal, which is what makes them dangerous: a year spent
lobbying the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors for a formal inquiry into the
Emporium's "unlicensed proliferation of dangerous magical assets"; the careful, deniable courting
of the city's anonymous Masked Lords, one at a time, into a bloc that needs no author; rival trade
offers aimed squarely at Triboar, Yartar, and Larkspur's restored Library, all timed to bleed the
Emporium's goodwill exactly when it can least afford to look weak. Bastion has not lost a single
one of those fights yet — not by matching Wimbleblat blow for blow, but by refusing to fight on
the terms Neebo assumes he must. He invited the Watchful Order's scrutiny rather than resisting it.
He answered the confederation's gold to Triboar and Yartar by simply reminding both towns that
Serpent escorts had already ridden out for them for a year without asking anything back. He has
quietly begun assembling his own dossier on the confederation's members and has, so far, used
none of it. Whether that restraint is virtue or patience, even Zoey isn't sure.
Elminster and Laeral watch all of this and say almost nothing to Wimbleblat at all — not
because Neebo is wrong, but because the confederation is asking them to rule against family, and
they have never quite been able to think of Flynn any other way. Laeral scolds him like the son she
never had time to raise properly; Elminster needles him like a father worried a clever boy is about
to be cleverer than is good for him. It was Elminster, not Wimbleblat, who said the truest thing
anyone has said about the Emporium all year — if you are pinned, the next move will be the
skewer — and he said it to Flynn's face, in private, the way you warn someone you love rather
than someone you're prosecuting. Flynn has never called Elminster anything so plain as father. He
doesn't have to. It's there in the way he goes quiet and actually listens — the one voice in the
world he never once tries to charm.
So Laeral's authority as Open Lord has, so far, kept Wimbleblat's confederation from becoming an
official censure, and Elminster's private warnings have kept Flynn from a few mistakes he might
otherwise have made in the dark. But neither of them has told Wimbleblat he's wrong to worry.

Places
Waterdeep — Seat of the Emporium and the Lord's Alliance.
Thornhold Keep — The Emporium's original northern stronghold on the High Road, its old
walls now sitting eastward — nearest the road — and humbled beneath the new Citadel to the

Things & concepts
Mythallar — Netherese orb of raw magic able to raise cities; its creation cleaved magic,
matter, and time and frayed the Weave. The Emporium hunts the lost ones — though how
many survive, and where, is unknown even to it.
The Moon Orb — Artifact said to be held by Larloch at Warlock's Crypt; Flynn's company
believes it is in truth a mythallar, and the Fables' next objective.
The Pact Primordial — The primordial agreement of creation between Jazirian and
Asmodeus, broken by the Mythallars; Flynn seeks to mend it.
Flynn's Wish — The reality-binding wish Hariqua granted, its exact workings clouded (by
divine power or the laws primordial). Flynn wished for access to Ahriman's power to outpace
his sibling and mend the Pact first; it misfired. It now binds the brothers for the first time in an
epoch — hiding Flynn from Ahriman's sight — and hints at a missing fulcrum, with
something foreboding standing between them.
The Infinite Planar Cycler — Del Thristen's machine balancing Dante (light pole) and the
Macroscian (dark pole) to draw limitless mana.
The Fireflies (of the Deepglow/Shimmerlake) — Underdark species whose patterns help
locate Wingsong Tower; the object of the Kost/Zhentarim race.
The Fables' Fortune — The Emporium's flying ship, captained by Gypsa, now mounting the
captured Soulfire Forge for extra raw firepower and flight speed.
The Soulfire Forge — A fire-magic artifact of the Circle of Flame, seized by Flynn himself
after the Fables drove Hariqua back to the Fire Plane. Zoey and Gypsa have since engineered it
into the Fables' Fortune, giving the ship greater firepower and speed.
The Mantle of the Raven Queen — Cloak gifted to Ty'Ny at Wingsong Tower, carrying a
charge: to hunt the undead, and to stand against any shadow that would bend death's power to
infernal ends.
